01Stimulus· research vision

Nima Mirkhani, PhD · Computational neuroscientist, University of Oxford

Steering brains back to healthy dynamics.

Brain disorders can trap neural activity in unhealthy states. I want to read those states from the signals we can record, such as EEG and fMRI, and use models to design stimulation that works as a well-timed nudge, guiding the brain into a healthier state. Not forcing it there. Helping it switch.

03Soma· integrating the inputs

How a well-timed nudge changes the brain, now and later

Four connected lines of work, from the instant a pulse arrives to the network changes that follow.

Instantaneous response

Phase-locked stimulation

Dynamical-systems models of coupled oscillators that predict how a neuronal population responds to pulses delivered at a specific phase of its rhythm, validated against biological recordings.

Long-term adaptation

Plasticity after stimulation

Predictive-coding models of how repeated stimulation reshapes connectivity, tested with analyses of single-cell recordings and population time series.

Learning rules

Dendrites as distributed predictors

Extending predictive-coding models of associative memory with dendritic predictors, and measuring what that means for robustness and information loss.

Translation

Subject-specific stimulation

Turning models into plans for non-invasive stimulation such as TMS, including adaptive, closed-loop protocols designed for long-lasting effects.
